Best Software for Drainage Contractors: An Honest Comparison

What drainage contractors actually need from software, how the main categories differ, and an honest account of what ScapesHQ does and doesn't do yet.

September 11, 2026 · 8 min read

Most "best software for contractors" articles are affiliate pages written by people who have never priced a trench. They rank tools by feature-count tables and put whoever pays the most at the top.

This one is written by people who build one of the options, which you should factor in. So here's the deal: this page is organized around what drainage work actually demands, and it says plainly where ScapesHQ is the wrong choice. If we're not a fit, we'd rather you know now than churn in April.

What drainage work demands that generic tools miss

Drainage sits awkwardly between trades, which is why so much contractor software fits it badly.

  • It's not recurring service. Lawn care, pest control and pool service software is built around routes, recurring visits and subscription billing. Drainage is project work — one large job per customer, sometimes years apart. Route optimization is worthless to you.
  • It's not a quick-quote trade. A drain cleaning company quotes over the phone. You do a diagnostic site visit, sample soil, check grade, and build a scoped estimate with depth bands. Software that assumes a flat-rate price book doesn't fit.
  • The proposal does the selling. A homeowner comparing $14,000 bids is buying an explanation. Multi-option proposals with photos and scope language aren't a nice-to-have; they're the product.
  • Customers aren't flat. The caller, the signer, the property manager, the address you'll come back to in three years. A single contact row loses all of it.
  • Leads arrive from Meta at all hours. And they go cold fast. Where those leads land is a bigger determinant of revenue than any feature in the estimating module.

Score any tool against those five and the field narrows quickly.

How to actually choose

Ignore feature counts. Answer four questions:

  1. What's your actual bottleneck? Leads going cold and jobs not closing → proposal-and-CRM. Money and invoicing → field service. Job costing at scale → landscape enterprise. Nothing is broken yet → keep your spreadsheet.
  2. How many people need the same truth? One person can run on anything. Three or more need shared records with roles.
  3. What does your customer structure actually look like? Mostly one-off homeowners → a flat model survives. Property managers, repeat customers, multiple addresses → you need a real relationship model.
  4. Can you leave? Ask every vendor how you export everything and how you close the account. The answer tells you how they think about ownership.

The switching-season warning

Whatever you choose, don't implement it in April. Contractors switch systems during their busiest month, discover the migration takes longer than promised, and end up running two systems through peak season.

Move in the slow months, with a real deadline for turning the old system off. If you're mid-season and something's broken, fix the single worst break — usually where leads land — and do the full move in the off-season.

FAQ

What's the best CRM for a French drain business?

There isn't a universal answer, and the honest framing is which bottleneck you're solving. If it's lead response and closing, a proposal-focused CRM fits. If it's invoicing and payment collection, a field service platform fits better today. If it's job costing across multiple crews, look at the landscape enterprise tools.

Do I need drainage-specific software?

Not literally — you need software whose shape matches project work with diagnostic selling. Generic field service tools built around recurring routes are the poorest fit regardless of what industry list they appear on.

How much should contractor software cost?

Compare total cost including per-seat charges, add-on modules and implementation, not the headline number. Then weigh it against one job — if a system prevents one lost $14,000 drainage job a year, the arithmetic is rarely close.

Should I run two systems?

Temporarily, during a migration, with a hard end date. Permanently, no — dual entry decays fast and you end up trusting neither.

How often does this comparison change?

Constantly. Verify pricing and current features with each vendor directly; this page is reviewed twice a year and last updated in October 2026.

The short version

Drainage is project work with diagnostic selling, so tools built around recurring routes fit it badly no matter how well reviewed they are. Pick by bottleneck: closing → proposal CRM, money → field service, job costing → enterprise landscape. And ask every vendor, including us, what they don't do yet — the ones with a real answer are the ones telling you the truth.
